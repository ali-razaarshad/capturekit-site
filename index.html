Privacy pledge

100% local by default

No accounts. No sign-in. No analytics, no telemetry, no fingerprinting. Captures live in your browser, not on our servers — because we don't have any.

Optional sharing services (Slack, Google Drive) are off until you turn them on, and each is a separate toggle with a plain-English privacy note. Turn the master switch off and nothing reaches the network.

Cloud AI (new in 1.8) is the one feature that sends a capture anywhere, and only if you switch it on and paste your own API key for Anthropic, OpenAI or Gemini. It talks to them directly — we have no server in between and never see your key or your captures. The editor tells you before the first upload, and Smart Redact never uses it.

Frequently asked

Questions we hear

Is it really free?

Yes. No paid tier, no ads, no upsells. It's built by one developer who wanted a better full-page screenshot tool and shipped it.

Do you see my screenshots?

No. There's no server on our side. Everything happens in your browser. Files leave your device only if you download them, copy them, or explicitly enable and click a sharing service (Google Drive uploads to your account, not ours).

What's an "editable PNG"?

Every PNG CaptureKit exports carries a compressed metadata chunk with your shapes and base image. It looks like a normal PNG to every other tool. But if you drop it back into CaptureKit later, the whole annotation session comes back editable — pen strokes, text, callouts, everything. JPEG, PDF, and SVG exports can't be reopened in CaptureKit (SVG keeps shapes editable in vector apps instead).

Does Smart Redact upload my screenshots?

No — Smart Redact runs entirely on your device. The OCR model (Tesseract WASM + English trained data, ~8 MB) and the face detector (face-api.js SSD MobileNet v1, ~5.6 MB) are both bundled inside the extension and loaded from your local install the first time you click Smart Redact. Nothing is sent to any server, no results are stored beyond the shapes you choose to accept, and the whole feature is off until you turn it on in Settings → AI features. For fastest performance, keep Chrome's hardware acceleration on (chrome://settings/system) — face detection runs 10–30× faster on GPU than on CPU.

Can I teach it to find my company's own sensitive data?

Yes, from 1.8. In Settings → AI features → Smart Redact you can add your own rules — a keyword like ACME-, or a pattern like ACME-\d{4} — each with a label that shows on the review chip. You can also list values that should never be flagged, and switch off any built-in detector that is always wrong for your screenshots. Skip the same kind of proposal three times and the editor offers to stop suggesting it. Rules export to a small JSON file, so a team can share one set without an account or a server, and they stay on your computer: a list of what you consider secret is sensitive in itself.

Does anything ever leave my device?

Only if you ask for it, and the list is short. Sharing services (Slack, Google Drive, Imgur) upload a capture when you click their button, and each is off until you enable it. Extra OCR languages download a language file from a public CDN when you click Download — that request carries nothing about you. Cloud AI (new in 1.8) uploads the capture you are working on to Anthropic, OpenAI or Google Gemini, using an API key you supply; it is off until you switch it on and paste a key, your browser asks permission before the first request, and the editor names the provider before the first upload. Everything else — capturing, editing, recording, exporting, Smart Redact, history — happens on your computer, and there is no CaptureKit server for it to reach.

Can I share settings — or captures — with my team?

Two ways, both new in 1.8 and both off by default. Sync (Settings → Sync) carries your preferences, style presets and shape templates between the browsers you are signed into, using the browser's own sync — your Google, Microsoft or Mozilla account — so there is still no CaptureKit account and no server of ours. Your API keys, captures, recordings and history never sync, and your Smart Redact rules only do if you switch that on separately. For captures, name a folder under Google Drive and every upload goes into it; share that one folder from Drive and your team sees everything you put there, with Drive's own permissions. CaptureKit asks Google for the narrow drive.file scope, so it can only ever touch the files and folders it created — which is also why each person uploads to their own folder rather than into a teammate's.

Can it record video?

Yes, from 1.8. Pick Record video in the popup, then choose what to record: This tab starts on the page you're on straight away, or Choose… hands over to your browser's own sharing prompt, where you can pick another tab, a window or your whole screen. Keep the sound and your microphone if you want them, then trim the clip and download a WebM video or an animated GIF. Firefox always asks through its own prompt. Recording, trimming and GIF conversion all run in your browser, and recordings stay in the extension's storage on your device until you delete them. Recording stops by itself after 2 minutes by default (up to 10 in Settings).

Can it screenshot something outside the browser?

Yes, from 1.8. Pick Desktop in the popup and your browser asks what to capture — another tab, an app window, or a whole screen. CaptureKit takes a single frame and opens it in the normal editor. It needs no new permission: the browser’s own picker is the consent step, and it appears every time, so nothing can be captured without you choosing it first. Your countdown setting applies, giving you a few seconds to bring the right window forward.

Does it give me time to get ready before recording?

Yes. A 3, 2, 1 countdown runs before recording starts, so you can switch to the window or screen you actually want. Nothing is captured during it, so the countdown never appears in the video, and Esc cancels. When you record a window or your whole screen the count also shows in the floating controls, which stay above every other window — so you can watch it while you move. Set it to Off, 3, 5 or 10 seconds in Settings.

Can I save a recording as MP4?

Yes. The player offers WebM, MP4 and GIF. MP4 is H.264 video with AAC sound — the combination PowerPoint, Keynote, Premiere, phones and Windows Photos all accept, where a WebM often bounces. The conversion happens entirely in your browser, frame by frame, with no upload and no ffmpeg to install; it just takes a little longer than WebM, which is the recording's native format. Any trim and any annotations are baked in. MP4 needs an H.264 encoder, so it is available in Chrome and Edge; Firefox keeps WebM and GIF.

Can I draw on a recording?

Yes. The player has its own toolbar: arrows, boxes, pen, marker, text, numbered steps, blur, pixelate, redact and spotlight — the same kit the screenshot editor gives you. Each annotation shows for the whole clip by default, or you can set it to appear only between two moments, so a label turns up exactly when it is needed. Blur and pixelate read the frame that is playing, so a box over something that moves keeps covering it. Everything is painted into the WebM, the GIF and any frame you save, and it all happens on your device.

Does CaptureKit ever download anything?

Only if you ask it to. The OCR that powers Smart Redact reads English out of the box. In 1.8 you can add other languages in Settings → AI features → More OCR languages. Each one is a 1–3 MB language file fetched from the jsDelivr CDN only when you click its Download button, checked against a fingerprint built into the extension, and stored in your browser. The request carries nothing about you or your screenshots, and text recognition still runs on your device. Remove deletes a language again. Everything else CaptureKit needs is inside the extension.

What is Smart Caption and where does the AI actually run?

Smart Caption adds a Caption button to the editor that generates a one-sentence description of your capture (useful as alt text or a filename) plus a ✨ Improve button that rewrites annotation text with a shorter, clearer, or more formal tone, and Ask… (new in 1.8), a small chat where you can ask questions about the capture ("What does this screenshot show?", "Any errors?"). It uses Chrome's built-in on-device AI (Gemini Nano) — a browser-managed model that runs locally on your machine. No image, text, question, or answer ever leaves your device; there is no network call. These features stay hidden unless the API is available in your browser and you enable them in Settings → AI features → Smart Caption. Requires Chrome 128+ with the built-in AI enabled — Firefox, Safari, and older Chrome simply don't show the buttons.

How does the Google Drive integration work?

It uses Chrome's built-in Google sign-in with the drive.file scope — the most restrictive Drive scope. CaptureKit can only see files it creates itself, never your other Drive contents. The scope, the sign-in, and the upload are all opt-in.

Does full-page capture work on Jira, Notion, Gmail, Linear?

Yes. Those apps don't scroll the whole browser window — the content lives inside an inner scroll container. CaptureKit detects that pattern automatically and drives the inner container instead, so Full page captures the whole ticket / doc / thread instead of just the first viewport. A quick prewarm pass loads any lazy images and embeds before stitching starts, so the finished image is one continuous shot with no duplicated rows.

Which browsers are supported?

Chrome, Edge, Firefox, and other Chromium-based browsers (Brave, Opera). Install from the Chrome Web Store, the Microsoft Edge Add-ons store, or Firefox Add-ons. Google Drive sharing is Chrome/Edge only — Firefox lacks the identity API that powers it. Smart Caption, ✨ Improve and Ask… need a browser with the built-in Prompt API (currently Chrome); elsewhere those buttons simply don't appear. Every other feature works the same on all three.
